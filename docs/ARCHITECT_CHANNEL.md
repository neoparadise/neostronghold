## 1. Executive Summary

The luxury residential smart home market in Cape Town is controlled by two gatekeeper professions: **architects** (who specify structural and electrical implications) and **interior designers** (who specify user-facing technology, finishes, and brand selection). No premium smart home system reaches a high-net-worth client without passing through one or both of these filters.

This document outlines a structured channel strategy to:

- Secure specification of our smart home platform by the top 10 architecture and interior design firms in Cape Town.
- Achieve CPD accreditation through SAIA (architects) and IID (interior designers) to unlock direct presentation access to decision-makers.
- Establish a legally compliant, ethically sound trade partner program with transparent commission structures aligned to SACAP and IID codes of conduct.
- Build developer partnerships for bulk specification in new-build luxury estates.
- Execute a show house strategy to place our technology in front of end-clients at the point of purchase.

**Primary Objective:** 15 specified projects within 12 months, generating ZAR 4-6M in hardware revenue per project at wholesale.

**Secondary Objective:** Become the default specified smart home system for Cape Town's top 5 architecture firms.

## 2. The Architectural Specification Process

### 2.1 Project Stages (SACAP / SAIA Standard)

Understanding the RIBA/SACAP work stages is critical to timing the approach. The specification window closes at Stage 4.

| Stage | Name | What Happens | Tech Relevance |
|-------|------|-------------|----------------|
| 0 | Strategic Definition | Client brief, site selection | No entry |
| 1 | Preparation & Brief | Project budget, high-level scope | Can plant seed |
| 2 | Concept Design | Interior designer engaged, mood boards, system outline | **Best entry point** — interior designer recommends systems |
| 3 | Spatial Coordination | Layouts finalized, MEP routes planned | **Second entry** — architect evaluates structural implications |
| 4 | Technical Design | Full spec written, tender documentation | **Drop-dead deadline** — if not in spec here, you are excluded |
| 5 | Manufacturing & Construction | Build | Only substitution if specified system fails |
| 6 | Handover | Commissioning, training | Aftermarket only |

### 2.2 Who Decides What

| Decision | Decision-Maker | When |
|----------|---------------|------|
| System brand selection | Interior designer (primary), architect (secondary) | Stage 2-3 |
| Structured cabling / conduit paths | Architect + Electrical Engineer | Stage 3-4 |
| Rack location, ventilation, power | Architect + Electrical Engineer | Stage 3-4 |
| User interface placement (keypads, touchscreens) | Interior designer | Stage 2-3 |
| Integration with lighting, HVAC, audio | Interior designer with AV consultant | Stage 2-4 |
| Security system integration | Security consultant + Architect | Stage 2-4 |

### 2.3 The Entry Strategy

1. **Before Stage 2:** Build relationship with architect/interior designer through CPD events, lunches, or warm introductions.
2. **Stage 2:** Interior designer is open to product exploration. Provide look-and-feel materials, finish samples, case studies. No pricing required yet.
3. **Stage 3:** Provide full system specification including cabling schedules, power requirements, and rack elevations. Architects need this for MEP coordination.
4. **Stage 4 Deadline:** Ensure your system is named in the electrical and AV sections of the technical documentation. Once written in, costly to remove.

### 2.4 Key Risk

If the electrical engineer or AV consultant has a preferred supplier relationship (many do with legacy brands), they will block substitution. **Win the spec writer, not just the architect.** The spec writer is often an independent electrical engineer appointed by the architect.

## 4. CPD Accreditation Guide

### 4.1 Why CPD

- SACAP (South African Council for the Architectural Profession) requires registered architects to earn **CPD credits annually** (typically 25-30 credits).
- SAIA (South African Institute of Architects) validates and accredits CPD courses.
- Interior designers registered with **IID (Institute of Interior Design Professionals)** also require CPD.
- A CPD-accredited course **guarantees attendance** — practitioners need the credits.

### 4.2 Step-by-Step: SAIA CPD Accreditation

#### Step 1: Prepare the Course Content
Develop a 1-hour presentation titled:

> *"Specifying Smart Home Technology in Luxury Residential: Structural, Electrical, and Aesthetic Considerations"*

Learning objectives must be explicit and measurable:
1. Identify the structural requirements for smart home infrastructure in new-build luxury residences (conduit paths, rack locations, power provisioning).
2. Evaluate the MEP coordination points between smart home systems and building services (lighting, HVAC, security, audio).
3. Assess the aesthetic integration strategies for user interfaces (keypads, touchscreens, sensors) in architecturally sensitive spaces.
4. Apply a specification checklist for Stage 3-4 technical documentation.

#### Step 2: Identify Validating Body
- **Architects:** Submit to SAIA (admin@saia.org.za) or a recognized SAIA regional chapter (Cape Institute for Architecture — capensaia.co.za).
- **Interior Designers:** Submit to IID (info@iidprofessions.co.za).

#### Step 3: Prepare Submission Pack
Required documents:
- Course outline with timings (60 minutes)
- Speaker CV/s (must demonstrate subject matter expertise)
- Learning objectives
- Sample slides (10-15 as PDF)
- Assessment method (multiple-choice quiz at end)
- Venue and date proposal
- CPD credit value proposed (typically 1 credit per hour)
- Fee per attendee (recommended: free for attendees — the goal is access, not course revenue)

**Submission fees:** SAIA charges approximately ZAR 2,000-5,000 per course accreditation (valid for 12 months).

#### Step 4: Accreditation Timeline
- **Submission deadline:** 6-8 weeks before proposed course date
- **Processing time:** 3-4 weeks
- **Approval valid:** 12 months (can present multiple times)

#### Step 5: Presentation Logistics
- Host at your showroom (if you have one) or offer to present at the firm's office
- Maximum 20-25 attendees per session for interaction
- Provide printed CPD certificates at the end (signed, dated, with SAIA/IID accreditation number)
- Digital certificates sent within 48 hours

#### Step 6: Annual Renewal
- Update course content annually with new case studies, technologies, and regulations
- Re-submit for accreditation 8 weeks before expiry

### 4.3 Dual Accreditation (Architects + Interior Designers)

- The same course can be submitted to **both SAIA and IID** with minor adjustments to framing.
- Architects need more structural/MEP content. Interior designers need more aesthetic/brand content.
- Consider **two variants** of the same hour:

| Variant | Focus | Validation |
|---------|-------|------------|
| Variant A | Structural & electrical implications | SAIA (architects) |
| Variant B | Aesthetic integration & client experience | IID (interior designers) |

### 5.2 What to Present

**Slides 1-3:** The Smart Home Market in Luxury Residential
- Market growth data
- Client expectations at ZAR 15M+ property level
- The cost of retrofitting vs. specifying at Stage 3

**Slides 4-8:** Technical Content (CPD Core)
- Infrastructure planning: conduit sizing, routing, cable types, rack locations
- Electrical coordination: power requirements, UPS, load shedding considerations
- Structural considerations: ceiling access for sensors, wall construction for keypads
- Integration points: lighting (DALI, 0-10V), HVAC (thermostat, zoning), shading (motorized blinds)
- Security integration: alarm, CCTV, access control convergence
- Audio: distributed audio, home theatre, multi-room

**Slides 9-12:** Aesthetic Content
- Finish options, colors, materials for keypads and touchscreens
- Flush-mount vs. surface-mount considerations
- Customization capabilities for unique architectural features
- The invisible technology philosophy

**Slides 13-15:** Case Studies
- Before/after of completed luxury homes
- Technical drawings showing integration
- Client testimonial (video if possible)

**Slides 16-18:** Support & Partnership
- Our commissioning process
- After-sales support structure
- Trade partner program overview

- One keypad (wall-mount, flush) — ideally wired and functional
- One touchscreen (tablet or wall-mount) showing live UI

## 6. Trade Partner Program

### 6.1 Commission Structure

#### Ethical Framework

SACAP Code of Conduct (Section 4.3) and IID Code of Ethics require:
- Full disclosure of any financial interest in specified products
- Transparency of commissions, discounts, or referral fees to the client
- Written documentation of the commercial arrangement
- No commission that could reasonably appear to influence professional judgement

#### Recommended Model: Disclosed Discount / Finder's Fee

The cleanest approach is a **trade partner discount** that the specifier can choose to pass through to the client or retain as a fee. This is declared in the client quotation.

| Role | Discount off List Price (Retained by Trade Partner) | Declared to Client? |
|------|----------------------------------------------------|---------------------|
| Architect | 5-10% | Yes |
| Interior Designer | 10-15% | Yes |
| Developer (bulk) | 15-20% (negotiated per project) | Yes |
| Electrical Engineer | 5% (referral only, not spec) | Yes |

**Alternative Model — Finder's Fee:**
For situations where the specifier does not want to manage the discount:
- 5% of total hardware value paid to the specifier as a finder's fee
- Declared as "referral fee" on client quote
- Paid on project completion and settlement

## 8. Show House Strategy

### 8.1 Why Show Houses Work

- Interior designers execute show houses to attract new clients
- Show houses are visited by 500-3,000 high-net-worth individuals over 4-8 weeks
- Technology in a show house generates organic inquiries from visitors
- Show house participation builds credibility as a "luxury brand"

### 8.2 How to Get In

**Approach 1: Partner with the Design Lead**
- Identify the interior designer leading the show house
- Offer technology at cost or subsidized (50-70% discount) in exchange for prominent branding
- Provide installation labor at cost
- Showcase one "hero" feature (e.g., invisible audio, automated shading)

**Approach 2: Sponsor the Show House**
- Annual show house events in Cape Town:
  - **Cape Town Festival of Architecture** (biennial, next: 2027)
  - **Design Indaba** (associated show houses)
  - **FNB Art Cape Town** (luxury partner activations)
  - **V&A Waterfront** developer show apartments
  - **SA Home Owners** magazine show house (annual)
- Sponsorship cost: ZAR 50,000-150,000 depending on level
- ROI measured in qualified leads generated

**Approach 3: Developer Show Unit**
- Developer model (Section 7): hardware at cost
- Ongoing show unit (no removal date) = maximum ROI

### 8.3 Show House Checklist

- [ ] Confirm show house dates (set-up and tear-down)
- [ ] Sign participation agreement (confirm what you provide, what designer provides)
- [ ] Provide hardware at agreed pricing (cost, subsidized, or free)
- [ ] Installation by our team or designer's contractor (our supervision required)
- [ ] Commission and programme (basic scenes, lighting schedules, music)
- [ ] Branded signage at entrance (one sign + small plaques in key rooms)
- [ ] QR code on keypad bezel linking to "Design Your Smart Home" inquiry form
- [ ] Leave-behind brochures in the entrance hall
- [ ] Post-show house: all leads entered into CRM within 48 hours
- [ ] Debrief with designer (what worked, what didn't, mutual referrals)
